# Addendum: the search chooses the number of tanks along

## Why

The maintainer widened #935: the search also picks the number of tanks along (2, 3 or 4), screened by damage. On the 140 × 29 × 12 m coastal LNG reference design, the governing damage case is a side breach across the single hold bulkhead between the two tank rows. It floods both holds from side to side. With 3 along, 13.0 m tanks pass IGC 2.7 (21,658 m³, against 16,832 m³ for 2 along). With 4 along, 13.28 m tanks pass (20,913 m³). Fewer, larger holds are the wrong lever; more, shorter holds are the right one.

## D1. The search space

- A. Outer loop over {2, 3, 4}; the existing walk (depth, tanks across, diameter) runs inside each. **Recommended.**
- B. Add tanks along to the one flat candidate list. Mixes the orders and breaks the memo and per-pair stop of design Q2.

A typed tanks-in-line input still pins the loop to that one value. A typed value is the designer's choice; the search must not override it. With it pinned, the result and the damage verdicts are as the design describes. The result says whether the count was typed or chosen.

## D2. Cost control

- Order: stability and the non-damage trace first, damage last (design Q2 unchanged). Run the loop in order 2, 3, 4, so the profile's default is always evaluated first.
- Early exits: a candidate that fails the cheap checks never runs damage. Within one (count along, depth, across) triple, stop at the first damage pass. Skip a count along if its best cheap-checked volume cannot beat the best damage-passing volume found so far. The bound uses only the cheap checks, so it is safe.
- Memo: key the damage verdicts by (tanks along, depth, across, OD to 1 mm). Reuse them in the 0.1 m refinement.
- Time budget: **recommended** is a cap on damage runs, not on wall time (a wall-time cap gives different answers on different machines). Default 24 runs; on reaching it, return the best pass so far and say "search stopped at the damage-run limit". If nothing has passed, report the closest, as design Risks says. The count stays in `damage_runs`.
- Open: is 24 the right cap? Measure on the example in PR 2 and report.

## D3. The objective

Most cargo volume among the damage-passing candidates. Ties (equal to 1 m³) go to the **fewest tanks**, then the fewest tanks along, then the larger diameter. Fewer tanks means less insulated surface, fewer bulkheads and fewer penetrations. This is **recommended**. Alternative: fewest tanks first, then cargo. It would pick 2 along on any design where 2 along passes at all, even at a large volume loss. Rejected: the owner's aim is cargo.

## D4. Hold bulkheads and end spaces

Equal hold lengths, one bulkhead between adjacent tanks, so 3 along has 2 hold bulkheads and 4 along has 3. The two end spaces keep the profile's current fore and aft rule; the length left for holds is the length between them. Tank length follows from the diameter and the hold length as it does today, and the length-over-diameter check still applies. **Recommended.** Alternative: unequal holds to move a bulkhead off the breach. It adds a free variable with a wide search; defer it. The side-breach case is generated by the existing case builder (`damage_cases.rs`) for each bulkhead position, so no new case code is needed. A test asserts that the case set follows the count.

## D5. The default

The profile's default stays 2 along. The search still evaluates 3 and 4 when the input is unpinned, so a design that today gets 2 along may change. The two coastal gas example Options will move: more cargo and a different tank count. Options pinned to 2 along, or on `igc-2g` 2.0.0 or below (no damage screen), do not move. Every moved Option is named in the PR body through `tools/design_impact.py changelog`. Re-pinning is by running the code.

## D7. Dependencies

Build after #987 and #988. #987 makes a breach flood a tank at its steel, not its insulation; #988 sends the breached cargo out at the intermediate stages. Both change which diameters pass at each count, and the figures above (13.0 m, 13.28 m, the volumes) predate them. Build on them: every pin and snapshot in 2b would otherwise be re-pinned twice, and the tie-break test could pick a different winner. Until both merge, this addendum's numbers are findings, not test values. Re-measure the three counts after they land, and put the new numbers in the PR body. PR 1 (move to core) has no dependency and may go first.
