# Manning rules for concept design — research synthesis

**Research line.** *Searched:* IMO A.1047(27), STCW 2010, MLC 2006 (as amended to 2018), UK MSN 1868 (M) Am.1, Liberia MAN-001 Rev 11/22, other flag pages, CCNR RPN 2026 Ch. 18–19, ADN and Dir. 2017/2397 (not opened), published crews of small gas carriers. *Exists:* two clear flag scales (UK, Liberia) by GT and kW; the RPN inland tables; MLC cook and rest rules. *Reusing:* those tables as data, in a rule of construction. *Building because:* no published method turns vessel particulars into a crew number and roles for concept design. Asked for by the maintainer in round 1 (2026-10-31).

Date: 2026-10-31. Scope: concept and preliminary design only. Not for approval or a Safe Manning Document.

**Status key**
- **Sourced** — primary text read in this session (instrument itself).
- **Flag copy** — flag-state notice read in this session (national guidance, not the convention).
- **Secondary** — a third-party page, or a national document quoted for a convention rule.
- **Known, not re-read** — standard rule text I did not re-open this session. Verify before use.
- **Derived** — my calculation from sourced inputs.

**Watch-system implication (Derived, rests on A-VIII/1 + A.1047 Annex 2 §1.3.2):**
- 24 h continuous navigation, 4-on/8-off → **3 bridge watchkeepers**. If the master must be watch-free (A.1047; Liberia ≥3,000 GT) → chief mate + 2 OOW.
- 6-on/6-off (two-watch) gives 12 h rest/day and 84 h/week, so it passes the rest-hour test arithmetically. Flags allow it at smaller sizes or near-coastal (UK NC ≥3,000 GT = master + CM + 1 OOW). It leaves no slack for cargo or mooring work, so peak-load (A.1047 §1.4.1) often breaks it.

## 6. Synthesis for a tool (proposal, labelled inference)

Everything in this section is **Inference** unless marked. Each step cites the rule it rests on.

### 6.1 Rule of construction

**Step 0 — Pick the regime.** (Inference from RPN 19.07 and MLC Art. II)
- SOLAS/load-line seagoing ship (incl. coastal-and-river): flag scale + STCW + MLC. On CCNR waters add RPN 19.07 checks.
- Inland vessel certificate only (river-only): RPN Ch. 19 (or the national inland rule) + ADN. MLC not applicable → no MLC cook rule; cabins by ES-TRIN.

**Step 1 — Inputs.** GT (ITC 1969: GT = K1·V, K1 = 0.2 + 0.02·log10 V — Known, not re-read), propulsion kW (flag interpretation for diesel-electric — flag it as a user input), trading area (unlimited / near-coastal / river), UMS yes/no, daily navigation hours H, cargo (gas/oil/dry).

**Step 2 — Bridge watch system** (rests on A-VIII/1 rest rules and A.1047 Annex 2 §1.3.2):
- H ≤ 12 and night alongside/at anchor with port watch: 2 watchkeepers.
- H > 12 (continuous): 3 watchkeepers (4-on/8-off). Allow 2 (6/6) only where the flag scale shows it (UK NC; Liberia ≤3,000 GT).
- Master watch-free if GT >3,000 (Liberia §3.3) → watchkeepers = CM + OOWs; else master may stand one.

**Step 3 — Deck officers** = master + watchkeepers (Step 2), floor = flag table (MSN 1868 Annex A or MAN-001).

**Step 4 — Engineers** by kW band and UMS (MSN 1868 Annex B; MAN-001): 750–3,000 kW UMS → C/E + 2/E; ≥3,000 kW unlimited → +1 EOOW (UK); non-UMS → +2 EOOW +1 AB engine (MAN-001 note 4).

**Step 5 — Ratings** by GT band (MAN-001 table): deck = AB(II/5) + II/4; engine = III/5 + III/4. Where the band grid has a hole, take the **higher** of the two neighbours (Inference; precaution, A.1047 Annex 2 §1.4).

**Step 6 — Cargo/gas role.** Default +1 "cargo engineer" for gas carriers as **owner option**, flagged as not rule-driven (STCW V/1-2 changes training only).

**Step 7 — Cook.** If seagoing and Steps 3–6 total ≥10 → +1 qualified cook (MLC A3.2(5) as read by Liberia/Singapore). Below 10 → galley trained-person rule only, no extra head.

**Step 8 — Owner margin.** Optional steward, ETO, bosun, cadet berths. Report separately from the minimum.

**Step 9 — Cross-check.** Show both UK and Liberia results; flag differences >1 person. Compare with reference ships (§4).

**Step 10 — Accommodation count** = minimum + owner margin + supernumeraries/pilot/riding crew. In inland mode B, one cabin per person if rest is taken underway (RPN 18.02(4)).

### 6.2 Worked case (a) — 140 × 29 m coastal LNG barge, 2.4 MW, coastal-and-river, 24 h

**GT estimate (Derived; depth not given).** Assume D = 6–12 m, block-like hull Cb_v ≈ 0.9, plus ~3,000 m³ of deckhouse and tank domes. V ≈ 25,000–47,000 m³ → K1 ≈ 0.288–0.293 → **GT ≈ 7,000–13,500**. ### 6.3 Worked case (b) — same vessel, 14 h daytime river operation

Two answers, because the regime decides:

**(b1) Kept under its seagoing certificate (Inference).** Flag scales do not reduce for daytime-only trade unless the flag grants restricted-operation manning (MAN-001 §3.3 allows fewer watches for "limited voyages"; no number given). - Bridge: H = 14 > 12 → in practice 2 navigating watchkeepers + night port watch. Proposal: master + CM + 1 OOW = **3** (= UK NC floor).
- Engineers 2, deck ratings 3–4, engine ratings 1–2 → **9–11**; cook only if ≥10.
- Planning figure **11–13**. Needs flag agreement; mark "flag dispensation required".

**(b2) Under an inland certificate (Rhine/ES-TRIN), mode A1, L >86 m (Sourced table, Inference on application):**
- S2: BM + helmsman + apprentice = **3**; S1: 3 or 4.

- RPN prescribes **no engineer**. For a 2.4 MW gas-electric gas carrier, propose **+1 engineer as owner choice**.

### 7.2 US 46 CFR — what the text says

**Structure (Sourced).**
- The Certificate of Inspection (COI) sets the minimum complement. The OCMI decides it, using
  laws, Part 15, and listed factors: size and type, equipment, route and port calls, cargo,
  service, automation, labour-saving devices, organisation (46 CFR 15.501(a)–(b)). There is **no
  crew table** in Part 15.
- Towing vessels: the COI states "minimum manning requirements" (46 CFR 136.205); the vessel must be
  manned per the COI, and the manning rules are in Part 15 (46 CFR 140.205(c)).
- Tank vessels: the OCMI enters the complement on the COI (46 CFR 31.15-1). Tank barges need not be
  manned unless the OCMI judges it necessary (46 CFR 31.15-5).

**Numeric floors that do exist (Sourced).**

| Item | Rule | Citation |
|---|---|---|
| Master | Required on every self-propelled inspected vessel and every seagoing documented vessel ≥200 GRT; a towing vessel ≥8 m needs a Master of Towing Vessels (or a >200 GRT master with a TOAR) | 15.805(a)(1),(2),(5) |
| Mates, inspected seagoing | ≥1,000 GRT: **3** (2 if voyage < 400 miles); 100–<1,000 GRT: **2** (1 if 100–<200 GRT and voyage ≤24 h); <100 GRT: 1. OCMI may raise; Commandant may reduce | 15.810(b)(1),(3),(4), (e), (f) |
| Mates, towing | The person navigating a towing vessel ≥8 m holds Mate (Pilot) of Towing Vessels or equivalent; no number | 15.810(d) |

| Towing operator hours | Master or mate (pilot) of a towing vessel ≥8 m: not more than 12 h in 24 h; read to permit two watches regardless of voyage length (where the 1936 convention does not apply) | 15.705(d); 46 U.S.C. 8104(h) |
| Able seafarers | Vessels ≥100 GRT: ≥65 % of the deck crew (officers excluded) are able seafarers; 50 % where two watches are allowed | 15.840(a) |

**ATB-specific text (Sourced).**
- 46 CFR 10.107 defines an ATB as a tug–barge pair with a hinged connection allowing independent
  pitch; ITBs split into dual-mode and push-mode. No Part 15 section gives ATB numbers.
- Pub. L. 117-263 §11508 (note to 46 U.S.C. 8104): the Coast Guard must allow an amended COI
  **without engine room watch setting** for inspected towing vessels certificated before
  19 July 2022 that form part of an ATB and carry accepted control and monitoring systems. It changes
  engineers, not deck crew. Its work instruction CVC-WI-037(1) refused the fetch (Not found).
- NVIC 2-81 Change 1 (25 Feb 1981), encl. (1) §6: sample ITB scales "for general informational
  purposes only". Push-mode (≥1,000 GT combined): master + 3 mates + 3 engineers + 4–6 AB + 0–2 OS
  (+ radio officer per FCC), plus room for 3 oilers during proving. Dual-mode tug 300–<1,000 GT:
  master + 2 mates + chief engineer + assistants as required + 2–4 AB + 1–2 OS. Dual-mode < 200 GT:
  2 operators. It cites statutes since recodified, so treat it as history (Inference).
- MSM Vol. III Part B Ch. 2 Section W "sample manning scales" for Subchapter M towing vessels exists
  (seen in search only) but refused the fetch (Not found).

**Reading (Inference).** The US set is a list of floors plus an inspector's decision. A tool can
compute the floors (master, mate count, chief engineer, watch count, able-seafarer share, tank PICs)
but not the complement. It needs **GRT** (regulatory tonnage), not ITC GT, plus route, voyage miles,
inspected or not, towing or not, and hours per day.

### 7.5 Are tug and ATB rules simpler?

**Short answer: no — not for a seagoing ATB tug, and only partly inland.**

1. **Liberia.** No tug or ATB table. The §12.2 table is headed "All except Offshore MOUs"; a
   tug enters by its own GT and kW (MAN-001 §12.2). The tug gets the same grid
   at a lower band: 10 + cook against 12 + cook for hull A. Not simpler — the same rule.
2. **UK.** No tug table. MSN 1868 §8.2 names only *harbour* tugs and inshore craft, and leaves them
   to working pattern, rota and call-out duties. A seagoing tug uses Annexes A and B by GT and kW.
3. **CCNR inland.** Pushers have a table (19.03) — but it needs more inputs than 19.02 (assembly
   length and breadth, number of pushed barges) and adds an engineer in groups 4–5. Other tugs have
   no table: the inspection body decides (19.06). Partly simpler: one table for pushers, none for
   tugs.
4. **US.** Fewer and blunter floors for towing: one Master of Towing Vessels, a Mate (Pilot), two
   watches on voyages under 600 miles (8104(g)), a 12-hour operator limit (8104(h)). But the crew
   number is the OCMI's decision on the COI (15.501, 136.205), and the sample scales (MSM Vol. III)
   could not be read. An ATB adds two rules, not fewer: tank-barge PICs (15.860) and the §11508
   engine-room-watch relief for older ATB tugs. Simpler to state, not simpler to compute.

**Inference for D13.** The spec's choice holds: run a seagoing ATB tug on `liberia-man-001` by the
tug's own GT and kW. Inland pushers on `ccnr-rpn-19` Art. 19.03. A US set could be a **check-only**
Rule Set of floors (master, mates, C/E, watches, AB share, tank PICs), never a crew generator,
because Part 15 holds no complement table. No ATB rule found treats tug and barge as one unit for
crew; the US combined-tonnage scale is a 1981 ITB example only (NVIC 2-81 §6.a(1)).
