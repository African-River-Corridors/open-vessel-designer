# Manning rules for concept design — research synthesis

**Research line.** *Searched:* IMO A.1047(27), STCW 2010, MLC 2006 (as amended to 2018), UK MSN 1868 (M) Am.1, Liberia MAN-001 Rev 11/22, other flag pages, CCNR RPN 2026 Ch. 18–19, ADN and Dir. 2017/2397 (not opened), published crews of small gas carriers. *Exists:* two clear flag scales (UK, Liberia) by GT and kW; the RPN inland tables; MLC cook and rest rules. *Reusing:* those tables as data, in a rule of construction. *Building because:* no published method turns vessel particulars into a crew number and roles for concept design. Asked for by the maintainer in round 1 (2026-10-31).

Date: 2026-10-31. Scope: concept and preliminary design only. Not for approval or a Safe Manning Document.

**Status key**
- **Sourced** — primary text read in this session (instrument itself).
- **Flag copy** — flag-state notice read in this session (national guidance, not the convention).
- **Secondary** — a third-party page, or a national document quoted for a convention rule.
- **Known, not re-read** — standard rule text I did not re-open this session. Verify before use.
- **Derived** — my calculation from sourced inputs.

**Watch-system implication (Derived, rests on A-VIII/1 + A.1047 Annex 2 §1.3.2):**
- 24 h continuous navigation, 4-on/8-off → **3 bridge watchkeepers**. If the master must be watch-free (A.1047; Liberia ≥3,000 GT) → chief mate + 2 OOW.
- 6-on/6-off (two-watch) gives 12 h rest/day and 84 h/week, so it passes the rest-hour test arithmetically. Flags allow it at smaller sizes or near-coastal (UK NC ≥3,000 GT = master + CM + 1 OOW). It leaves no slack for cargo or mooring work, so peak-load (A.1047 §1.4.1) often breaks it.

## 6. Synthesis for a tool (proposal, labelled inference)

Everything in this section is **Inference** unless marked. Each step cites the rule it rests on.

### 6.1 Rule of construction

**Step 0 — Pick the regime.** (Inference from RPN 19.07 and MLC Art. II)
- SOLAS/load-line seagoing ship (incl. coastal-and-river): flag scale + STCW + MLC. On CCNR waters add RPN 19.07 checks.
- Inland vessel certificate only (river-only): RPN Ch. 19 (or the national inland rule) + ADN. MLC not applicable → no MLC cook rule; cabins by ES-TRIN.

**Step 1 — Inputs.** GT (ITC 1969: GT = K1·V, K1 = 0.2 + 0.02·log10 V — Known, not re-read), propulsion kW (flag interpretation for diesel-electric — flag it as a user input), trading area (unlimited / near-coastal / river), UMS yes/no, daily navigation hours H, cargo (gas/oil/dry).

**Step 2 — Bridge watch system** (rests on A-VIII/1 rest rules and A.1047 Annex 2 §1.3.2):
- H ≤ 12 and night alongside/at anchor with port watch: 2 watchkeepers.
- H > 12 (continuous): 3 watchkeepers (4-on/8-off). Allow 2 (6/6) only where the flag scale shows it (UK NC; Liberia ≤3,000 GT).
- Master watch-free if GT >3,000 (Liberia §3.3) → watchkeepers = CM + OOWs; else master may stand one.

**Step 3 — Deck officers** = master + watchkeepers (Step 2), floor = flag table (MSN 1868 Annex A or MAN-001).

**Step 4 — Engineers** by kW band and UMS (MSN 1868 Annex B; MAN-001): 750–3,000 kW UMS → C/E + 2/E; ≥3,000 kW unlimited → +1 EOOW (UK); non-UMS → +2 EOOW +1 AB engine (MAN-001 note 4).

**Step 5 — Ratings** by GT band (MAN-001 table): deck = AB(II/5) + II/4; engine = III/5 + III/4. Where the band grid has a hole, take the **higher** of the two neighbours (Inference; precaution, A.1047 Annex 2 §1.4).

**Step 6 — Cargo/gas role.** Default +1 "cargo engineer" for gas carriers as **owner option**, flagged as not rule-driven (STCW V/1-2 changes training only).

**Step 7 — Cook.** If seagoing and Steps 3–6 total ≥10 → +1 qualified cook (MLC A3.2(5) as read by Liberia/Singapore). Below 10 → galley trained-person rule only, no extra head.

**Step 8 — Owner margin.** Optional steward, ETO, bosun, cadet berths. Report separately from the minimum.

**Step 9 — Cross-check.** Show both UK and Liberia results; flag differences >1 person. Compare with reference ships (§4).

**Step 10 — Accommodation count** = minimum + owner margin + supernumeraries/pilot/riding crew. In inland mode B, one cabin per person if rest is taken underway (RPN 18.02(4)).

### 6.2 Worked case (a) — 140 × 29 m coastal LNG barge, 2.4 MW, coastal-and-river, 24 h

### 6.3 Worked case (b) — same vessel, 14 h daytime river operation

Two answers, because the regime decides:

**(b1) Kept under its seagoing certificate (Inference).** Flag scales do not reduce for daytime-only trade unless the flag grants restricted-operation manning (MAN-001 §3.3 allows fewer watches for "limited voyages"; no number given). A moored/anchored LNG ship still needs a port/anchor watch overnight (A.1047 Annex 2 §1.1.11, STCW A-VIII/2 — Known). So:
- Bridge: H = 14 > 12 → in practice 2 navigating watchkeepers + night port watch. Proposal: master + CM + 1 OOW = **3** (= UK NC floor).
- Engineers 2, deck ratings 3–4, engine ratings 1–2 → **9–11**; cook only if ≥10.
- Planning figure **11–13**. Needs flag agreement; mark "flag dispensation required".

**(b2) Under an inland certificate (Rhine/ES-TRIN), mode A1, L >86 m (Sourced table, Inference on application):**
- S2: BM + helmsman + apprentice = **3**; S1: 3 or 4.
- + ADN gas expert: usually the boatmaster holds it → +0 (else +1) (RPN 14.01).
- If LNG-fuelled: boatmaster + bunkering crew LNG experts (RPN 15.01) → qualification, not head.
- RPN prescribes **no engineer**. For a 2.4 MW gas-electric gas carrier, propose **+1 engineer as owner choice**.
- Planning figure **4–5**; no MLC cook.
- Caution: a 140 × 29 m unit exceeds Rhine/ES-TRIN norms for many waterways; the RPN numbers are a reference scale, not proof of admissibility.

## Round 3: inland and tug manning sets (2026-10-31, from this note only)

The maintainer (round 2): build manning Rule Sets like class rules; Liberia the coastal default; which inland
sets, and are tug and ATB sets simpler?

- **Inland:** CCNR RPN Ch. 19 is the one sourced, tabled set (§3.2: crew by length group, mode A1/A2/B
  and equipment standard S1/S2). Ghana publishes no manning scale (§2). Dir. 2017/2397 sets
  qualifications, not crew numbers (known, not re-read). Proposed inland default: `ccnr-rpn-19`.

- **Seagoing ATB tugs:** the flag tables apply by the tug's own GT and kW (Liberia MAN-001, UK MSN
  1868); a small tug falls into their lowest bands. US 46 CFR (Subchapter M, Part 15) runs its own
  route-based regime; not read (§2 row: "poor fit for a generic tool").
- **Simpler?** Partly. Inland pushers have a direct table. Seagoing tugs use the same flag tables
  as the barge hull, just at small GT and kW. Not yet verified: US towing-vessel rules, and whether
  Liberia treats an ATB pair as one unit. Not yet researched on the web.
